Foreshocks

In the months leading up to the Loma Prieta earthquake, several smaller tremors rattled the region. These foreshocks were subtle warnings of the stress building along the San Andreas Fault.

Main Shock

On October 17, 1989, the Loma Prieta earthquake struck with a magnitude of 6.9. The main shock caused significant shaking in the San Francisco and Monterey Bay areas, collapsing freeways and buildings.

Aftershocks

In the hours and days following the main event, numerous aftershocks rippled through the region. While smaller than the mainshock, these quakes kept residents on edge as the ground continued to move.

Rebuilding

Once the seismic activity settled, attention turned to recovery and rebuilding. The Loma Prieta earthquake led to improved building codes and a greater awareness of earthquake preparedness in California.
