# 1989 Loma Prieta Earthquake: 25 Multimedia Resources Database

The October 17, 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake struck at **5:04:15 PM Pacific Daylight Time**, measuring magnitude 6.9 and becoming the first major U.S. earthquake broadcast live on national television during the World Series. This curated database provides **25 direct-access multimedia resources** spanning video, audio, and photographs from the earthquake moment through the following days.

## Videos: Live broadcasts and documentary footage

### 1. FEMA/National Archives official documentary
**Direct URL:** `https://archive.org/download/gov.archives.arc.951729/gov.archives.arc.951729_512kb.mp4`  
**Alternative:** `https://archive.org/download/gov.archives.arc.951729/gov.archives.arc.951729.ogv`  
**Timestamp:** October 17, 1989, 5:04 PM PT and aftermath  
**Description:** Official federal documentary showing Candlestick Park during World Series, California Highway Patrol response to Bay Bridge and Cypress Structure damage, rescue attempts, and victim evacuations  
**Source:** National Archives (ARC 951729), FEMA National Emergency Training Center  
**License:** Public Domain (CC0)

### 2. KGO-TV/ABC six-hour live earthquake coverage
**Direct URL:** `https://archive.org/download/1989BayAreaEarthquake/videoplayback.mp4`  
**Timestamp:** October 17, 1989, beginning 5:04 PM PT  
**Description:** Six continuous hours of breaking news coverage from ABC affiliate KGO-TV, starting from the World Series broadcast interruption and continuing through the evening with field reports from damaged areas  
**Source:** KGO-TV/ABC San Francisco  
**Media Type:** Video (1.2 GB MP4)

### 3. Marina District amateur footage
**Direct URL:** `https://archive.org/download/1989.Loma.Prieta.Earthquake.Marina.District/1989.Loma.Prieta.Earthquake.Marina.District_512kb.mp4`

**Description:** 4 minutes 21 seconds of footage shot by Exploratorium employee showing Marina District immediately after the quake—pavement cracking from liquefaction, smoke rising from fires that would engulf the neighborhood, building collapse in progress  
**Source:** Michael Joseph Woody (videographer)  
**License:** CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 US

### 4. World Series broadcast - earthquake moment
**Direct URL:** `https://archive.org/download/videoplayback-2021-02-08-t-170217.759/videoplayback%20-%202021-02-08T170217.759.mp4`  
**Timestamp:** October 17, 1989, 5:04:15 PM PT (exact moment)  
**Description:** ABC World Series Game 3 broadcast capturing the exact moment the earthquake struck Candlestick Park during pre-game show. Al Michaels says "I'll tell you what, we're having an earth—" before feed cuts. Includes stadium shaking, 13 seconds of technical difficulties graphic, and subsequent coverage  
**Source:** Revelation Video (archived ABC broadcast)

**Timestamp:** October 18, 1989 (morning, day after earthquake)  
**Description:** News report by MG Perez documenting earthquake damage in downtown Santa Cruz near the epicenter, showing Pacific Garden Mall destruction where 7 people died  
**Source:** CBS 8 San Diego

### 6. Cypress Street Viaduct documentary
**Direct URL:** `https://archive.org/download/1-year-of-plainly-difficult-ray-wg-225-xy-i/A%20Brief%20History%20of%EF%BC%9A%20The%20Cypress%20Street%20Viaduct%20Collapse%20Disaster%201989%20%28Documentary%29%20%5ByJnB6g9N8-g%5D.mp4`  
**Timestamp:** Documentary covering October 17, 1989 events  
**Description:** Educational documentary detailing the I-880 Nimitz Freeway double-deck collapse that killed 42 people—the highest death toll from any single location. Includes engineering analysis, archival footage, and rescue operation documentation  
**Source:** Plainly Difficult documentary series

**Direct URL (Part 1):** `https://archive.org/download/casadt_000223/casadt_000223_a_access.mp3`

## Technical notes for implementation

All URLs in this database point directly to downloadable media files suitable for embedding in visualization projects. Government sources (USGS, FEMA, National Archives) are **Public Domain** and require no licensing. Internet Archive materials have individual licenses noted. Wikimedia Commons files use the standard `upload.wikimedia.org` direct file pattern.

**Attribution format for government images:** "Photo by [Photographer], U.S. Geological Survey, from USGS Digital Data Series DDS-29 (1999)" or "National Archives and Records Administration / FEMA"

For additional resources, the complete **USGS DDS-29 collection** contains 103 images accessible via the URL patterns provided, organized by geographic region from San Francisco through Monterey Bay.
