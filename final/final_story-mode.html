Foreshocks

In the months leading up to the Loma Prieta earthquake, a handful of smaller tremors rattled the region. To most residents they felt like ordinary California “little quakes,” but in hindsight they were hints of the stress building along the San Andreas Fault.

Main Shock

On October 17, 1989, at 5:04 pm local time, the Loma Prieta earthquake struck with a magnitude of 6.9. The shaking was strong enough to disrupt the World Series broadcast, collapse sections of freeway, and cause widespread damage across the Bay Area.

Aftershocks

In the hours and days that followed, dozens of aftershocks rippled through the region. Most were smaller, but together they paint a picture of how the fault adjusted after the main slip — a fading echo of the moment when the fault finally gave way.

Rebuilding

Once the seismic sequence calmed, attention shifted to inspection, repair, and redesign. The Loma Prieta earthquake helped prompt changes in building codes and a renewed emphasis on retrofitting vulnerable infrastructure around the Bay Area.
