01 — Timeline

Three Years of Seismic Activity

From June 1988 to June 1990, thousands of earthquakes shook the region. The mainshock on October 17, 1989 stands out dramatically.

Loading timeline data...

In this two-year window, an average day before the mainshock saw about 27 earthquakes. After October 17, that daily rate jumps to nearly 60 earthquakes per day—more than twice as many—and stays elevated for months, driven first by an intense burst of aftershocks and then by a long tail of ongoing activity.
02 — Scale

Thousands of Tiny Quakes,
One Giant Shock

Each dot below is one earthquake between June 1988 and June 1990. Most are tiny M<2.0 tremors that barely reach the surface, while a handful of larger events — including the M6.9 mainshock — tower above the crowd.

Loading magnitude data...

Out of all recorded earthquakes in this period, 25,466 — nearly 89% — were magnitude 2.0 or less, tiny tremors often unfelt by humans. Only 75 events exceeded magnitude 4.0, and just 7 (about 0.02%) reached magnitude 5.0 or higher. The scatterplot above makes this imbalance visible: a dense base of small quakes, with only a few larger dots rising toward the top.
03 — Depth

Shallow Swarms,
Deeper Shock

Earthquakes in this sequence did not all occur at the same depth. Plotting magnitude against depth reveals a cloud of shallow, small events near the surface and a deeper M6.9 mainshock that ruptured lower in the crust.

Loading depth data...

On average, earthquakes in this sequence occurred about 5.4 km (≈3.3 mi) beneath the surface. Most events cluster within the upper 10 km (≈6 mi) of the crust as tiny M2–3 tremors. The M6.9 mainshock, by contrast, struck deeper at about 17.2 km (≈10.7 mi), near the base of this swarm — a deeper rupture that then drove thousands of shallower aftershocks above it.
04 — Geography

Across the whole sequence, epicenters trace the San Andreas fault system, with almost one-third of all earthquakes occurring within roughly 30 km of the mainshock and nearly half within about 80 km. Depth Patterns

Earthquakes don't just vary in location — they occur at different depths.

Among earthquakes with reliable depth estimates, about half occurred shallower than 5 km, and another 46% between 5–15 km. Only around 3% reached depths greater than 15 km — a band that includes the 17.2 km M6.9 mainshock at the base of this sequence.

07 — Data & Methods

How We Built This View

A brief look at how we collected, filtered, and transformed the data that powers this page.

Data comes from the USGS ComCat earthquake catalog for a box around the San Francisco Bay Area, between June 1, 1988 and June 30, 1990. We query all events in this window and then filter to records whose type is "earthquake", keeping only rows with valid magnitude and depth values.

Times are shown in UTC and centered on the 1989-10-18 00:04:15 mainshock. Energy estimates use the Gutenberg–Richter relation E = 101.5M + 4.8 joules, and TNT equivalents assume 4.184×1012 J per kiloton. This matches the pipeline in our DataDownloader_LomaPrieta.py script and ensures every visual on this page is backed by a reproducible method.
